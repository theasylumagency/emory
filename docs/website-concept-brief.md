# Emory Barber & Beauty — Website Concept Brief

## Goal

Design the first visual concept and high-level UX direction for the new Emory Barber & Beauty website.

This is not a typical beauty-salon website and should not look like one.

The website has three simultaneous jobs:

**1. Build a distinctive Emory brand world.**  
**2. Prove professional competence and trustworthiness.**  
**3. Move the visitor toward booking with as little friction as possible.**

The primary conversion is **booking an appointment**.

Do not treat the site as an online brochure.

---

## Brand Direction

Emory is a contemporary Barber & Beauty salon in Saburtalo, Tbilisi.

The brand should feel:

- urban
- local
- editorial
- confident
- slightly rebellious
- warm and human
- visually intelligent
- unpretentious
- professional without corporate language

Avoid the visual language of conventional beauty websites.

Do NOT rely on:

- beige luxury
- rose gold
- black-and-gold premium styling
- marble
- pink beauty aesthetics
- generic “self-care” imagery
- glamorous stock-model photography
- soft spa visual clichés
- slogans such as “discover your beauty” or “your beauty is our passion”

Emory should feel closer to an independent editorial / cultural / urban brand than to a traditional salon website.

The UI itself should remain clean and easy to use. The personality should come from photography, typography, composition, copy, cropping, motion and unexpected moments — not from decorative clutter.

---

# Main User Journey

The user should move naturally through this sequence:

**Who is Emory?**

↓

**What do I want today?**

↓

**Can they actually do this well?**

↓

**Who should I trust with it?**

↓

**What will it approximately cost?**

↓

**Book.**

The booking CTA must remain accessible throughout the journey.

On mobile, a persistent but discreet bottom booking action is appropriate.

---

# Website Structure

Keep the site deliberately compact.

Primary navigation:

**Services**  
**Work / Cases**  
**Team**  
**Contact**  
**Book**

There is no need for a conventional standalone “About Us” page.

There is also no need for a separate generic “Prices” page. Pricing belongs inside services and relevant cases.

Primary pages:

- Home
- Services
- Work / Cases
- Team
- Contact
- Booking flow

---

# Homepage Structure

## 1. Header

Minimal.

Logo on the left.

Navigation should feel lightweight and editorial.

Booking should remain visually distinct but not look like an aggressive e-commerce CTA.

---

## 2. Hero

Large human-led photography.

Do not use a salon interior as the main visual.

Avoid predictable beauty poses.

The hero should immediately establish Emory as a place with character.

Content should remain minimal:

**Emory — Barber & Beauty**

A short brand line — placeholder for now.

**Givi Kartozia St. 10 · Delisi**

**Book**

Hero can occupy most or all of the first viewport.

Photography is more important here than explanatory text.

---

## 3. “What do you want today?”

Immediately after the hero, move to user intent.

Possible categories:

**Haircut**  
**Color**  
**Barber**  
**Nails**  
**Care / Treatment**  
**I don’t know yet**

Do not organize the main service architecture primarily as “Women / Men”.

The important behavior is:

The user selects what they are trying to achieve.

“I don’t know yet” is a real service path, not a joke or secondary link.

It should lead toward consultation.

This section can use large typography, image-led blocks, or an unconventional grid. Avoid a standard set of six identical SaaS-style cards.

---

## 4. Featured Real Case

Show one strong case prominently.

Not just before/after.

Explain:

- what the client wanted
- what problem or limitation existed
- what Emory proposed
- what was actually done
- approximate time
- stylist
- approximate price if appropriate

Primary CTA:

**“I want a similar result”**

This CTA is preferable to a generic “Book now” in case-related contexts.

The goal is to communicate expertise through decision-making, not only visual transformation.

---

## 5. Cases / Work

Show several real situations rather than a conventional gallery.

Examples of case labels:

- repairing an unsuccessful blonde
- changing shape without losing length
- first short haircut
- keeping grey hair natural
- correcting tone
- reducing damage instead of doing the requested procedure

Each image should ideally carry a reason/story.

The website should answer:

**Why is this result good?**

Instagram already shows attractive pictures. The website must add judgment and expertise.

---

# Ninutsa

Ninutsa is an AI-created Emory character and part of the brand universe.

She is NOT:

- the salon owner
- a real customer
- a testimonial source
- an employee
- a virtual receptionist
- the central subject of the entire website

She should appear occasionally and unexpectedly.

Think of her as a recurring editorial character who inhabits the Emory world.

Possible use:

A full-width interruption between functional sections.

Example:

Large photograph of Ninutsa.

**“Just the ends.”**

Small caption:

**Ninutsa, every single time.**

Then a subtle link to haircut services.

Her appearances should feel like cultural/editorial moments rather than advertising placements.

The website must remain recognizably **Emory**, not “Ninutsa’s website”.

---

# Team Section — Signature Moment

Do NOT design conventional stylist cards with:

photo  
name  
job title  
years of experience

This section should be one of the most distinctive moments of the site.

Working direction:

## “Who would you go to if…”

The user discovers specialists through situations rather than titles.

Examples:

**“Someone ruined my color.”**  
→ Ana

**“I know I want a change, but I don’t know what.”**  
→ Tako

**“I want the Pinterest photo — exactly.”**  
→ A specialist who first tells you whether it is actually possible.

Possible structure:

Use one large editorial group scene of the real Emory team rather than identical studio portraits.

The people should feel caught in a real Emory moment:

- someone working
- someone talking
- someone looking toward the camera
- someone drinking coffee
- someone interacting with a client or colleague

Names can appear as subtle interactive markers.

On hover/click/tap, show the specialist through:

**Name**

**Specialization**

**People usually come to them when…**

Use personality and professional judgment instead of CV language.

A good stylist profile should communicate:

> “This is the person I want for my specific situation.”

An optional secondary idea worth exploring:

### “Choose a chair”

Each stylist can be connected to a real working chair/station in the salon.

This can become an interaction or visual motif, but only if it remains intuitive and does not become a gimmick.

---

# Professional Positioning

One recurring Emory value should be:

**A good professional sometimes says no.**

For example:

If a requested color will unnecessarily damage the person’s hair, the stylist should say so.

If a reference image is unrealistic for the person’s current condition, explain that.

This is not negative messaging.

It is part of the trust proposition.

Emory should feel like a salon that is trying to achieve the best outcome, not simply sell the largest possible service.

---

# Reviews / Trust

Include real external proof.

Do not create fictional testimonials.

The visual presentation should be strong and simple.

A rating or short selected real reviews may appear as a dedicated trust moment.

Avoid generic testimonial carousels.

---

# “I Don’t Know Yet” Conversion Section

This deserves its own strong block later on the homepage.

Concept:

**You know you want to change something.  
You just don’t know exactly what yet.**

That is normal.

Possible actions:

**Book a consultation**

or

**Send us a photo**

The user should not be expected to know professional salon terminology.

---

# Location Section

Keep it practical.

Show:

**Emory Barber & Beauty**  
**Givi Kartozia St. 10**  
**Delisi / Saburtalo**

Working hours — placeholder.

Actions:

**Open in Maps**  
**Call**

Prefer a real exterior / entrance / street image over another polished interior beauty shot.

The site does not need a large automatically loaded map embed.

---

# Photography Direction

Approximately:

**70% people**  
**20% meaningful details**  
**10% interior / environment**

Photography should feel real, editorial and contemporary.

Avoid:

- glossy stock photography
- perfectly centered poses everywhere
- heavy beauty retouching
- repetitive smiling
- overly luxurious staging
- uniform corporate portraits

Use:

- close crops
- movement
- imperfect framing
- skin and hair texture
- actual working moments
- real expressions
- strong editorial compositions

The visual world should allow Ninutsa and real Emory people to coexist naturally.

---

# Visual Design Direction

Explore:

- bold typography
- generous scale
- clean UI
- asymmetric editorial composition
- large photography
- unexpected crops
- controlled whitespace
- small moments of humor
- restrained animation
- strong mobile experience

The design may feel slightly rebellious, but it must never become difficult to navigate.

Usability beats visual cleverness whenever the two conflict.

Avoid over-designing.

Do not turn the website into an agency portfolio piece whose main purpose is to impress other designers.

It is a salon website whose design happens to be unusually good.

---

# Mobile

Mobile is critical.

The path should be even shorter than desktop:

**Hero → intent/service → one strong case → booking opportunity → additional proof**

Booking should stay accessible through a discreet sticky bottom action.

Avoid intrusive popups and floating circular buttons.

---

# Booking UX

Do not use a long conventional form.

Preferred structure:

### Step 1
What do you want?

### Step 2
Do you want a particular specialist?

Include:

**Any available specialist**

### Step 3
Choose date/time

### Step 4
Name + phone number

Finish.

Choosing a specific stylist must not be mandatory.

A new customer often does not know the team yet.

Exact booking software/integration is not decided at this stage.

Design the UX independently of the backend provider.

---

# Content Status

For the first concept, use realistic placeholders where final data is not yet available.

Still unresolved:

- exact service list
- exact prices
- final team roster
- final working hours
- final booking provider/integration
- final homepage brand line
- final real cases and case photography

Do not allow these missing details to block the design exploration.

---

# What We Want From This First Exploration

Do NOT attempt to deliver a polished production-ready website yet.

The priority is to establish:

1. visual direction
2. homepage rhythm
3. typography
4. photography behavior
5. service discovery pattern
6. team-section concept
7. Ninutsa’s role
8. mobile logic
9. booking visibility
10. the overall emotional character of Emory

We should be able to look at the result and answer:

**“Does this feel unmistakably like Emory?”**

and

**“Would a real customer understand what to do next?”**

Both must be true.

---

## Final principle

Do not make a more beautiful version of a normal beauty-salon website.

Create a website that feels like **Emory could only have made it this way**.
